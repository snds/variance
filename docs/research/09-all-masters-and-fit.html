All six masters, and fitting to a drawing

Research 08 reached about 90% of Google’s weight masters with Google’s own moves. This note runs the remaining passes: all six masters, a fit read straight off a drawing, angled stroke ends, and two tries at a better rule.

Follows research 08. Code: prototype/rebuild/segments.py, source.py, density.py in snds/variance.

  • The controls hold up on every axis. Fed Google's own moves, the generator reaches 89–95% at all six masters (wght, GRAD and opsz, both ends of each).
  • Fitting to a drawing works without Google's data. Reading the moves straight off a reference master, with no edge table, lands within 0–2 points of that ceiling everywhere. This is the measuring engine for the editor's "fit to a drawing" tool, and it works for any reference.
  • The learned rule is the limit, and it varies by axis. 85–88% at opsz 48 and GRAD −50, 70–76% at opsz 20, GRAD 200 and wght 100, 64% at wght 700. Heavier, denser masters are where Google's judgement varies most.
  • Angled ends don't close the end gap. Stroke ends are still the largest miss (4.5–6.6% of edge pixels), but cutting them at an angle scores up to 3 points lower on average than flat ends. The misses at ends are about junctions and end shape, not angle. That makes them a manual job for now.
  • The rule improved a little, then stopped. Trained on research 08's corrected edge table, the learned rule's per-edge match on these 20 icons goes from 69% to 74% (wght 100). Adding how much ink surrounds each edge adds under 1 point.

How the fit works

At each sample on our default outline (each stroke side, each stroke end corner, each solid edge), walk along the outward direction through the reference drawing and find the nearest place where its ink ends. That distance is the move. The median per segment becomes the segment's setting. An earlier version used the nearest edge in any direction; near junctions it picked up the wrong part (deselect at wght 100: 80% against 97%).

What this means for Variance

  • Ship fit-to-a-drawing as the core of the manual flow. A designer who draws a master once gets about 90% of it as source settings immediately. The editor then only needs to show the last 5–10%.
  • Treat stroke ends as a designer control. Neither a length change nor an angle captures them. Give the end handle a few shapes (flat, angled, follow the neighbour at a junction), and expect hand work there.
  • Use the learned rule per axis, and say how sure it is. On opsz 48 and GRAD −50 it is close to the ceiling; on wght 700 it needs review. The review queue should weight icons by axis.
  • Stop tuning the rule's features. The next gains come from designer fixes feeding back as training examples (the "learn from fixes" loop), not from more geometry inputs.

Caveats

  • The 69% → 74% per-edge figure counts any edge whose predicted move is within 0.25 px, so it isn't directly comparable to research 06's table, which counted only edges that moved parallel.
  • Fit scores read moves from the very master they are scored against, so they measure how well the controls can express a drawing, not a prediction.
