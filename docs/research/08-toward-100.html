Toward 100%

Research 07 put one anchor per stroke into the generator. This note makes the generator’s controls finer, fixes two measuring bugs that held research 06 and 07 back, and asks what is left for a designer to fix by hand.

Follows research 07. Code: prototype/rebuild/source.py, segments.py, orient.py in snds/variance.

  • With finer controls, the generator can reach about 90% of Google's edges. Fed Google's own moves, it now matches 94% at wght 100 and 89% at wght 700 (research 07: 77% and 61%). 18 of 20 icons pass 90% at wght 100, and 10 of 20 at wght 700.
  • The automatic rule improves too, but it's still the bottleneck. The learned rule alone now matches 76% at wght 100 and 64% at wght 700 (research 07: 67% and 55%). It beats research 07 on 16 of 20 icons at wght 100 and 14 of 20 at wght 700.
  • Two bugs were hiding about a third of the gain.
  • The edge search found a long straight edge only near its middle.
  • On 13% of Material's edges, the measured "outward" direction pointed into the ink. Material's overlapping contours don't all run the same way, so a grow read as a shrink there.
  • What's left is mostly stroke ends, plus moves no rule predicts. With Google's own moves, stroke ends hold the largest remaining miss (3–5% of all edge pixels), then stroke sides on curves (1–3%). The 13–25 point gap between the learned rule and Google's own moves is designer judgement. That gap is the job for the editor.

What changed in the generator

  • Each segment of a stroke moves its own sides. A fitted stroke often chains several segments that Google treats differently. Each segment now has its own left and right distance from the centre-line. A side may even cross the centre-line, as long as the stroke stays positive width. With equal moves everywhere, this is the same as an ordinary stroke (checked pixel for pixel).
  • Stroke ends move. Butt and square ends can get longer or shorter at each master. Round ends follow the width.
  • Solids and detail patches move edge by edge. Each outline segment of a filled part moves by its own amount. A badge can now shift or shrink, which one grow amount couldn't do.
  • Patches keep only what they change. A detail patch is trimmed to what it adds or removes at the default. That leaves the default rebuild exact, and the hidden overlap no longer pins ink in place at other weights. Thin slivers stay put; only thick, well-measured pieces move.
  • Corners are mitred only at real corners (over 10°). A smooth join that changes offset is bridged straight, so no spikes.

Ends are where Google redraws most: tapered or angled cuts, ends that shift along the stroke, and ends at a junction that change shape. A single length change can't express those.

What can't be automated

Three kinds of change come from a designer's eye, not a rule:

  1. Anchor choices the rule gets wrong. Which side holds still on a particular stroke. This is the 13–25 point gap above. The controls exist; only the choice is missing.
  2. End and junction shapes. Angled or tapered ends, and how two strokes meet when one gets heavier.
  3. Real redraws. A part moved for balance, a shape simplified at a light weight, a detail dropped when space runs out. Research 04 found these in under 4% of edges, but they are concentrated in a few icons.

Tooling for the manual part

The principle: every manual fix is stored as a parameter on the source, never as a second drawing. The source stays the only truth, so a fix survives when the skeleton changes, and it can teach the rule.

Tool What the designer does What is stored
Side pins Click a stroke segment's side at a master to hold it still or let it move. Drag to set an exact amount. A per-segment side move (research 08's sides).
End handles Drag a stroke end along the stroke at a master; pick an end shape (flat, angled, tapered). An end move, later an end angle.
Part nudge Move or scale a part (a badge, a dot) at one master. A transform on the part, per master.
Fit to a drawing Draw or import one master by hand, once. Variance fits its own controls to it, as this note did with Google's master. The fitted moves, so the drawing becomes parametric.
Point override Last resort: move outline points at one master. Per-point deltas, flagged stale if the skeleton changes (the decision in research 01).

Around those, three support features keep the work small:

  • Diff overlay and score. Show the master over a reference (blue and red, as in this note's figure), with the share of edges within 0.25 px. Click a miss to jump to the control that owns it.
  • Review queue. Rank icons by risk: where the rule is unsure, or where the generated master breaks a check (a counter closing, two parts closer than a gap, a stroke thinner than the minimum). Designers see the worst icons first instead of checking all of them.
  • Learn from fixes. Each pin, end move or nudge is a labelled example. Retrain the set's rule on them, so the next icon needs fewer fixes. For a new set this is the whole loop: fix 20 icons, retrain, review the rest.

"Fit to a drawing" is the strongest of these, and it already works: this note's "Google's own moves" results are exactly that fit. With a reference master, 18 of 20 of the hardest icons get to 90%+ with no hand edits at wght 100.
