Anchors in the generator

Research 06 learned a rule for which side of a stroke Google moves when the weight changes. This note builds it into the generator and regenerates the 20 hardest Material icons from research 03 at wght 100 and 700.

Follows research 06. Code: prototype/rebuild/source.py, learned.py in snds/variance.

  • The learned rule works end to end. On these 20 icons it matches more of Google's master than either earlier recipe, at both weights.
  • wght 100: 67% of Google's edges within 0.25 px, against 57% for research 04's table and 53% for centred growth.
  • wght 700: 55%, against 47% and 31%.
  • It wins on most icons. The learned rule beats the table on 15 of 20 icons at both weights, and beats centred growth on 16 of 20 (wght 100) and 20 of 20 (wght 700).
  • One move per stroke side has a ceiling. Even Google's own moves, reduced to one number per side, reach 77% (wght 100) and 61% (wght 700). Only 2 icons pass 95% that way. These are the hardest icons in the set. The rest needs finer control: an anchor per segment, per-part resize, and crowding.

What changed in the generator

  • A stroke can move each side by its own amount. At a master, a stroke's left side moves kL and its right side kR. The generator turns that into a new width (W + kL + kR) and a sideways shift of the centre-line ((kL − kR) / 2). The skeleton stays the source; the shift is computed per master from the rule, never stored as a second drawing.
  • Solids get one grow amount. Every edge moves the same distance.
  • Corners stay sharp. A shifted centre-line is offset segment by segment and the offset lines meet at their intersection (a mitre). An earlier, simpler version made long spikes at corners.
  • Booleans fall back harder. When Skia can't combine two shapes, the generator retries on copies snapped to 1/16, 1/4 and 1 unit.

  • Biggest wins: shuffle_on and repeat_one_on (filled squares with knocked-out arrows) go from 26–38% to 68–88%. The learned rule keeps the square's outside still and moves the arrows, which is what Google does.
  • Where it loses to the table: fingerprint_off and theater_comedy at wght 100, and padel at wght 700. All three are long curved strokes where one move per side is too coarse.

What this means for Variance

  • Use the learned rule as each stroke's default anchor. It is the best automatic recipe so far.
  • Next lever: anchors per segment, not per stroke. The fitter chains long strokes, so one stroke can hold several segments that Google treats differently. Splitting the anchor per segment raises the ceiling.
  • Then add per-part resize and crowding from research 04 to the same generator, and score all six masters.

Caveats

  • The learned rule was trained on all Material icons except these 20.
  • The score covers edges only (within 0.25 px). It doesn't check that the masters stay point-compatible; that is a separate step.
