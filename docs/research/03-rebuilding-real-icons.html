Rebuilding 40 hard icons

The 20 hardest Material Symbols and the 20 hardest IBM Carbon icons, rebuilt as Variance sources: centre lines, solids, knockouts and cut rules. Then checked against the originals, and for Material, against Google's own weight 100 and 700 masters.

Follows research 01 and research 02. Code: prototype/rebuild/ in snds/variance.

The short answer. At the default style, both sets can be matched exactly. 38 of 40 rebuilt icons match to within 1/20 of a pixel. The other 2 hit a bug in the geometry engine, not in the source format.

But an exact match is the easy part. Any icon can be matched by pasting in fixed shapes. What matters is how much of each icon is driven by rules, because only rules change correctly with weight. The automatic rebuild turned a median of 93% of Material's ink and 96% of Carbon's into rules. 31 of 40 icons reached 90%.

The big finding is about weight. No single rule reproduces Google's weight masters. The best one explains about 51% of the change at weight 100 and 14% at weight 700. Google draws each master with per-icon decisions. So "100% Material at every weight" needs two things research 01 didn't plan for: a per-part rule for which edge stays put, and hand-drawn override masters.

How the test works

  1. Pick the hardest icons. Every icon in both sets (4,004 Material, 2,569 Carbon) was scored for overlaps, gaps, holes, knockouts and solid parts. The top 20 in each set were used.
  2. Rebuild a source. A tool finds the centre lines of the strokes, fits straight lines and arcs, and decides what is a stroke, a solid or a knockout. It then tries cut rules (gap size and side) wherever they bring the result closer to the original.
  3. Patch the rest. Anything still different becomes a patch: a fixed shape that adds or removes ink. Thin patches are fitting tolerance along an edge. Thick ones mean the tool missed a real part.
  4. Check. Generate the icon from the source and compare it with the original, sampled every 1/40 px at 24 px. "Exact" means no difference thicker than 1/20 px. "Rules" is the share of ink not owned by patches.

The tool is automatic on purpose. It shows what a designer gets with no manual work, and where the source format needs the designer to say what they meant.

Rebuilds are also repeatable. Running the tool twice gives byte-identical sources. That took one fix: the centre-line finder breaks ties at random unless it is given a fixed seed.

Where the automatic rebuild falls short

Every low score traces to one of four gaps. None of them needs a new kind of part. Each needs the designer to state an intent that the outline alone doesn't carry.

GapExampleWhat fixes it
Dot or short stroke? A small square is as wide as a stroke. From the outline alone, it could be either.deselect, padelThe layer name says solid. A dot keeps its size as weight changes; a stroke end doesn't.
Gap baked into stroke ends. A slash's gap was learned as strokes that stop early, not as a cut. At a heavier weight the gap would close.notification--offThe slash's layer says cut gap=1 (research 02).
Organic curves. Freehand curves like fingerprints fell through the centre-line finder. (Whole rings did too, but that was a bug, fixed in research 05.)fingerprint_offIn a real source these are drawn as strokes, so nothing is guessed.
Filled shapes with knockouts. A solid heptagon with a cut-out wheel was patched instead of built as solid plus knockouts.logo--kubernetesLayer says knockout. Material does this well already: shuffle_on and repeat_one_on came out as a solid square with nine knockouts each.

Correction, later the same day: Carbon ships invisible helper shapes (fill="none") inside some filled icons. The first run counted them as ink for two icons, kubernetes--control-plane-node and microservices--2. With them removed, those two rose to 99% and 96% rules, and the Carbon numbers above include the fix.

Correction, from research 05: whole rings came out as zero-length arcs, a bug in the arc fitter. With it fixed, emoji_language rose from 52% to 94% rules, padel from 77% to 99% and portfolio--management from 38% to 95%. The numbers above include the fix.

The two engine failures (fingerprint-recognition and logo--kubernetes) are Skia's boolean operations giving up where two curves almost touch. A second failure of the same kind came from writing patch shapes with two decimals; six decimals fixed it.

Design styles the sources had to cover

  • Material: 2 px strokes, cut gaps around slashes, solid-with-knockout icons (shuffle_on, repeat_one_on), and filled details such as the bell's clapper.
  • Carbon: thinner 1.5 px-equivalent strokes, flat ends, one-sided cuts that only clear one side of a stroke, concentric arcs, and logos that are mostly solid shapes.

Carbon needed one thing Material didn't: cuts that clear one side only. The side=left|right option on a cut covered it.

Results across weight (Material only)

Carbon ships one weight, so this part only applies to Material. Each Material source was regenerated at weight 100 and 700 and compared with Google's own masters. Three ways to change weight were tried:

RecipeHow it worksWeight 100Weight 700
Centre strokesChange stroke width. Both edges move equally.25%9%
Grow the outlineNo source at all. Push the default outline in or out evenly.31%0%
Counters stay putEdges facing an enclosed space move little; the outside edge moves the rest. Numbers read off Google's masters.51%14%

Numbers are the median share of Google's change that each recipe reproduces. 100% would be a perfect match. 0% is no better than not changing weight at all.

What Google actually does

assignment_globe. Left: default against weight 100. Right: default against weight 700. Blue is ink only the default has; red is ink only the other weight has.

  • Counters are anchored. At weight 100 the inner edges barely move and the whole silhouette shrinks. At 700 the silhouette grows outward. Research 01's placeholder did the opposite (closed strokes grew inward). That placeholder is wrong for Material.
  • Lines move, not just thicken. The clipboard's text lines shift position between weights.
  • Details are redrawn. The globe's continents change shape. No offset rule produces that.

The same icon rebuilt at weight 700 with centre strokes, against Google's weight 700 master.

What this means for Variance

  1. Intent goes in layer names. Dot or stroke, cut or stroke end, knockout or hole: an outline can't say which. The layer-name grammar from research 02 needs solid, cut and knockout to be required where they apply, and the linter should flag likely misses.
  2. Add an anchor rule to strokes. Each stroke needs to say which edge stays put as weight changes: inner, outer or centre. Material anchors the counter side. Without this, no recipe gets past about half.
  3. Overrides are needed for Material parity. Redrawn details and moved lines can't come from rules. If the goal is to match Material at every weight, the override editor moves from "design for" into scope for the weight extremes.
  4. Our own style doesn't need Google's choices. A Variance icon set can define its own weight behaviour and get it from rules alone. Matching Google's masters is a choice about how close to Material we want to be.

Next tests

  • Add the anchor rule, then fit it per stroke against Google's masters. That shows how far rules alone can go.
  • Hand-build five of these icons as proper layered sources, with no patches, to confirm the four gaps above are the tool's and not the format's.
  • Fix the closed-ring case in the centre-line finder. Done in research 05.
