Closing the weight gap

Every icon in research 03 and 05 now rebuilds exactly at the default style. This note works on the next gap: matching a set's other weights without hand fixes. A dot primitive in the fitter, a strict weight test on Phosphor, and a test of whether Google's anchor choice can be predicted.

Follows research 05. Code: prototype/rebuild/extract.py (dot_part), weights.py, anchor.py in snds/variance.

  • Dots are now a primitive. A dot is a zero-length stroke with round caps, so it grows with the pen. All 540 rebuilds stay exact. Tabler icons with 90%+ of ink in rules: 138 → 146 of 150. Research 03 medians: 93% → 95% (Material), 96% → 97% (Carbon).
  • For a pen-style set, Variance already matches the real weights. Rebuild only Phosphor regular, change only the pen, compare with Phosphor's own thin, light and bold. Thin and light land within 0.25 px on 95%+ of edges for 98–100% of icons. That is as good as a perfect outline offset of the original, so the fitter adds no error.
  • What's left in Phosphor bold is Phosphor's own redraws. 54% of icons pass (an ideal offset: 59%). The rest have parts moved or resized by hand, the same kinds research 04 found in Material.
  • Google's anchor choice is partly predictable. A rule learned from half of Material, tested on the other half, matches 10–13 points more edge length than research 04's context rule at every master (wght 700: 53% → 64%). Icons fully matched at one master go up 2–6×.
  • It's about space, not keylines. Nearly all of the gain comes from measuring the room in front of and behind each stroke exactly, instead of in buckets. Position in the icon and the 20 px live area add about 1 point.

Dot primitive

A round component no wider than about two strokes is fitted as a dot: a stroke of zero length with round caps. A dot drawn larger than the stroke keeps the difference (extra), so it still follows the pen. Round holes of the same size become knockout dots.

Phosphor weight test

104 icons. Rebuild regular from its outline only, set the stroke to Phosphor's thin, light or bold pen (8, 12, 24 on its 256 grid), and compare with Phosphor's real file. "Ideal offset" grows or shrinks the original regular outline evenly: the best any pen-only method can do.

  • At the strict default-style test (nothing off by more than 1/20 px), few icons pass even for the ideal offset (thin 20 of 104). Phosphor's own weights aren't exact offsets at that scale, so 0.25 px is the fair bar here.
  • Bold misses are designer changes: folders and vibrate move whole parts. Only 6 icons pass with the ideal offset and fail with Variance.

Predicting Google's anchors

Research 04 measured all 172,000 Material edges at six masters. Here a gradient-boosted tree learns each edge's move from what the source knows (its context, space in front and behind, stroke thickness, facing, place in the icon and the live area, part size). It is trained on half the icons and scored on the other half, so it can't memorise an icon.

What this means for Variance

  • Ship the dot primitive. It's the last common fitter gap on stroke sets.
  • For pen-style sets, weights are solved. Changing the stroke width of the rebuilt source is as good as it gets. The rest is hand work the set's own designers did, which the override layer holds.
  • For anchored sets, replace the bucket table with a learned curve. Use it as each stroke's default anchor. The per-stroke override stays for the rest and now covers fewer edges.
  • Keylines don't explain Google's choices. Don't build a keyline rule.

Next tests

  • Put the learned anchor into the generator as a per-stroke default (a side offset of the centre-line), regenerate the 20 research 03 Material icons at wght 100 and 700, and score against Google.
  • Turn the learned rule into a few curves a designer can read and edit.
  • Learn Phosphor bold's per-part resizes the same way.
