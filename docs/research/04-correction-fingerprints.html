How Google corrects Material Symbols

Every edge of all 4,004 Material Symbols, measured between the default style and six of Google's own masters. Then sorted by what kind of correction Google made, and what it would cost us to make the same one.

Follows research 03. Code: prototype/rebuild/edges.py, fingerprints.py, tiers.py, risk.py, carbon16.py in snds/variance. Full ranking: prototype/rebuild/material-ranking.csv.

The short answer. Yes, there are patterns, and most of Google's corrections are cheap. Across the six masters, a fixed rule by context covers half to three quarters of all edges. A small number per icon or per part covers another 4 to 16%. A per-stroke anchor setting covers another 12 to 32%.

True redraws are rare. Under 4% of edges change shape at any master. That work sits in a short list: 159 icons have 10% or more of their edges reshaped, and 20 have 20% or more.

With anchors, resizes and a crowding rule, 63% of icons come within 0.25 px on at least 95% of their edges at every master. Rules alone get 5 icons there. Adding the anchor setting is the biggest jump.

Carbon is different. Its only redraws are 65 icons drawn again at 16 px. Material's patterns don't predict which of those Carbon changed, so each style needs its own masters to learn from.

How it works

  1. Same points, every master. In a variable font, every master has the same points in the same order. So each edge can be followed from the default style to weight 100 and 700, size 20 and 48, and grade −50 and 200.
  2. Measure each edge. For all 172,000 edges: how far it moved, whether it stayed parallel, and how the stroke width and the gap in front of it changed.
  3. Describe where it sits. What's in front of the edge (open space, a counter, a tight gap), what's behind the stroke, and whether it faces the icon's centre.
  4. Find the rule. For each context and master, the most common move in Google's font becomes the rule.
  5. Label the rest. Every edge the rule misses by more than 0.25 px is labelled by what Google did instead.

  • Weight moves the outside and keeps the counters. At weight 100 the outside edge comes in 1.3 px and counters don't move. At 700 the outside goes out about 0.85 px and counters close by 0.3 px. A lone stroke splits the change evenly.
  • Optical size does the opposite. At 48 px the silhouette stays put and strokes thin from the inside.
  • Grade grows the outside first. At grade 200, outside edges move 0.8 px and counter edges barely move.
  • Stroke width is very consistent. The width change itself is the same almost everywhere. What varies is which side takes it.

Crowding is predictable

At weight 700, a stroke with more than 4 px of space around it gains the full 1.15 px. A stroke beside a gap under 2 px gains about 0.9 px, and half of them gain less than standard. That's a rule we can build: stroke growth shrinks as the nearest gap shrinks.

IBM Carbon

Carbon has one weight, so there are no masters to compare. The closest thing is the 16 px set: 65 icons that Carbon drew again at 16 px instead of shrinking the 32 px version. At 16 px Carbon's stroke is 1/16 of the icon, the same share as at 32, so any difference is a deliberate redraw.

  • 22 of 65 were really redrawn. The rest are the 32 px drawing at half size.
  • The main move is shifting a part about 1 px so it lands on the 16 px pixel grid: the bell's base, the avatar's head, the dot of an exclamation mark, the top ring of "nominal". This is Material's resize fingerprint.
  • Small details change proportion: arrowheads and shaft ends are re-proportioned; the moon in "asleep" and the asterisk in "interactions" are redrawn.

Can Material's patterns predict Carbon?

We tested whether an icon's shape alone predicts how much hand work it needs, using the rates learned from Material.

  • Within Material, partly. It ranks icons by hand work with a rank correlation of 0.57. Of the 10% it flags as worst, 41% really are in the worst 10%, four times chance. It's weaker at spotting reshapes (0.29).
  • Across to Carbon, no. On the 65 Carbon pairs, the prediction runs slightly the wrong way. Carbon's 16 px fixes are about the pixel grid; Material's weight fixes are about space and anchoring.

So the fingerprint kinds carry over (resize, anchor, reshape), but the rates don't. A new style needs a few hand-made masters before we can predict its work.

What this means for Variance

  1. Build the context rules in. The table above becomes the default recipe per axis. It is the "a rule for which edge stays still" from research 03, now measured.
  2. Add an anchor setting per stroke. It's the biggest single lever (12 to 32% of edges). Default it from context, and let the designer flip it.
  3. Add a resize per icon and per part, per master. A scale and a shift. Cheap to store, and covers Carbon's 16 px fixes too.
  4. Add a crowding rule. Strokes beside tight gaps gain less weight.
  5. Keep overrides for the redraw list. Hand-drawn masters for about 159 icons, and spot fixes for about 544.

Next tests

  • Look for a rule that predicts Google's anchor choice (for example, keep the edge on the icon's keyline).
  • Ask whether Carbon has other internal sources of variation (for example its 20 and 24 px sets, which are tiny in this package).
